IDEAS

Things to do

Lake stuff isn't listed here — pontoon, kayaks, and paddleboards are already sorted, so it's a given. This is everything off the water, plus a few longer day-trip options for whoever wants a bigger adventure — most of the week should stay local so we maximize time together.

Camden village + coast

  • Windjammer sail★ 4.9, Tripadvisor's #1-rated attraction in Camden
    Schooner Surprise runs 2-hr day sails from Camden Harbor past Curtis Island Light, max 18 passengers — so we'd split across two sailings. $50–65/person. Book ahead for a group our size.
    TeensAdults
  • Mount Battie / Camden Hills State Park★ 4 on Tripadvisor
    Short steep hike or drive to the summit; 360° views over the harbor and bay. $6/person to drive up, parking at the top.
    All ages*
  • Curtis Island Light overlook
    Land-based view, no hike required — take Bay View St east from Route 1 to where it meets Beacon Ave, look for the small "viewpoint" sign and worn path down to a bench on the shore.
    Grandparents
  • Downtown Camden shops & harbor walk
    Flat, stroller/wheelchair friendly, easy half-day — Harbor Park and the amphitheater by the Town Docks, shops along Bay View, Main, and High St.
    GrandparentsKids
  • Farnsworth Art Museum, RocklandMixed-to-positive on Tripadvisor — strong for the Wyeth collection specifically
    ~20 min drive; one of the largest Wyeth family collections anywhere, good rainy/hot-day option.
    Adults

Day trip (1–1.5 hr drive)

  • Boothbay Harbor & Coastal Maine Botanical GardensTripadvisor's #1 attraction in Boothbay (2,100+ reviews)
    ~1 hr drive. New England's largest botanical garden — kids' garden with fairy houses and giant troll sculptures, butterfly house, harborside snack shack. Easiest full-family day trip of the bunch.
    All agesWhole day
  • Pemaquid Point Lighthouse905+ Tripadvisor reviews, #2 of 9 things to do in New Harbor
    ~1–1.25 hr drive. Classic Maine lighthouse on dramatic rock ledges, tide pools, a small Fishermen's Museum in the keeper's house. $2 parking fee. Pairs well with lunch in Damariscotta on the way.
    All agesHalf day
  • Monhegan Island
    ~45 min drive to Port Clyde, then a ~50–70 min ferry each way on the Monhegan Boat Line (one boat a day: 9:30am out, 4:15pm back — book ahead, ~$55/adult round trip). No cars on the island, spruce forest, dramatic cliffs, artist colony. The closest thing to last year's Acadia-style "hop in and go" adventure, just by boat instead of car.
    AdventurousFull day
  • Blue Hill, Deer Isle & Stonington
    ~1–1.75 hr drive. Working fishing village, art galleries, the Caterpillar Hill scenic overlook over Penobscot Bay. Doing the whole peninsula loop is a full 6-hour day — fine to just go as far as feels right and turn back.
    Scenic driveFull day
  • Acadia National Park / Bar Harbor
    ~2 hrs each way — a bit past the 1.5-hr window, so probably a repeat only for anyone who really wants to go back. Most of the group did this last year; the options above stay closer so more of the week is together at the house.
    OptionalLong drive

License age (freshwater)

Lake Megunticook sits right against the towering cliffs of Mount Megunticook on its east shore. It fishes well for warm- and cold-water species alike:

Largemouth bassSmallmouth bassRainbow trout Landlocked salmonBrown troutBrook trout Chain pickerelWhite perch

Access: Route 105 launch (southwest shore) — gravel ramp, shallow depending on water level, trailer parking across the road, carry-in/out friendly. Route 52 launch (east shore) — deeper water for bigger boats, less trailer parking. Both have seasonal public restrooms. Dock fishing right at the house is the easiest option for kids.

License: Maine requires a freshwater fishing license for anyone 16+ fishing inland waters — kids under 16 can fish unlicensed alongside a licensed adult. Non-resident options: 1-day $18, 3-day $30, 7-day $62, season $83. Buy online through Maine's MOSES system or at a sporting goods store in town.

What's biting & what to throw

  • Bass — steady lately207 logged catches on Fishbrain, incl. an 18", 4 lb largemouth
    The smallmouth/largemouth fishery is rated "very good" with above-average size fish, though largemouth numbers run lower than smallmouth. Topwater poppers work well early morning right up until the sun gets high, then switch to soft plastics or spinnerbaits worked deeper as it warms up through the day.
  • Pickerel & white perch
    This lake is specifically called out as having some of the best pickerel fishing in the area — easy action for kids off the dock. Live shiners or worms under a bobber work for both; perch will also hit small spinners and jigs.
  • Trout & landlocked salmon
    Rainbow, brown, and brook trout plus landlocked salmon are all present but fish deeper and cooler by August — trolling with streamers or spoons out past the drop-offs is the typical summer approach, better early morning or evening than midday.

Current catch limits (South Zone general law)

  • Bass (large- & smallmouth)
    2 fish daily, only 1 may exceed 14"
  • Brook trout
    2 fish daily (South Zone lakes), 6" minimum
  • Landlocked salmon
    2 fish daily, 14"–25"
  • Lake trout (togue)
    2 fish daily, 18" minimum
  • White perch
    25 fish daily
  • Chain pickerel
    10 fish daily, no minimum length

Megunticook only has smelt-specific special regulations (not relevant to what we'd be fishing for) — otherwise general South Zone law applies. Rules can change year to year, so double-check the current Maine IFW law book before keeping anything.
